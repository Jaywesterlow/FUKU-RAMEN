<overview>
How to write a cold mail that pulls the owner in. Read this before every mail. The examples show range; they are not templates. Copying their wording or their order breaks the variation rule in SKILL.md.
</overview>

<what_changed>
Drafts used to open on a fault and end on a link. Jaymar's words on those: "seems too much like spam", and "all they did was point out one part of the site that wasn't good and then tell them, okay, here's the site."

What he asked for instead: the owner should feel that someone went out of their way to make something for them, that it costs them nothing, and that a call follows. The mail should tell an actual story and get the owner a little invested before the link.
</what_changed>

<rejected_draft>
Sent back as spam-like. Keep it as the thing not to write.

```
Subject: your homepage

Hi Jakub,

Your homepage doesn't tell me when you're open. I had to click through to Menu & Info to find out, and most people won't.

So I built a new site for Fuku as a demo, with your own photos and the hours up front: https://fuku-ramen.vercel.app

Worth a look?
```

Why it fails:
- It opens on what is wrong. The owner is on the defensive in the first line.
- Nothing in it is about Fuku. Swap the name and it fits any restaurant.
- The demo arrives as a fix for a complaint, not as something made for them.
- It ends on a question, so the next move is theirs. Most will not make it.
</rejected_draft>

<accepted_draft>
Approved by Jaymar. Shown so you can see the four things at work, not so you can reuse it. "Over the past two weeks I studied" was true for that prospect only. Do not write it again unless the user says it is true.

```
Subject: built this for fuku

Hi Jakub,

Over the past two weeks I studied how ramen restaurants in Amsterdam present themselves online, and Fuku stuck with me. Six courses that end in a bowl is a story your current site barely tells.

So I built you a new one, with your photos and the evening course by course: https://fuku-ramen.vercel.app

It's yours to look at and costs you nothing. I'll call later this week to hear what you think.
```

Where the four things sit:
- Their story: six courses that end in a bowl.
- The gap: the current site barely tells it.
- The thing made for them: a new site, their photos, the evening course by course.
- No cost and the call: costs you nothing, I'll call later this week.
</accepted_draft>

<ways_in>
Pick one before writing. In a run with several prospects, do not use the same one twice in a row.

| Way in | What you need from the research | First sentence is about |
|---|---|---|
| Their concept | What they do that the neighbours do not | The idea behind the business |
| Their own words | A line from their menu, about page or an interview | The quote, given back to them |
| A photo | One image on their site or social feed | What is in the picture |
| The person | Where the owner trained, worked or came from | The route that led here |
| A number that is theirs | Years open, seats, courses, generations | What that number means |
| The place | The street, the building, the neighbourhood | Why here |
| The thing made for them | The demo itself | What they will see when they click |
| One dish or product | The thing regulars come for | That dish |

If the research gives you none of these, tell the user what is missing. Do not fill the hole with a compliment.
</ways_in>

<order_options>
The four things can come in any order that reads like a person wrote it.

- Story, gap, gift, call. Calm, the default for owners who do not know Jaymar.
- Gift, story, gap, call. Opens on the link's promise, then says why them.
- Story, gift, call, with the gap inside the gift sentence ("with the hours where a guest looks first").
- Person, story, gift, call. When the owner's own route is the strongest detail.

The gap may shrink to a clause. It may not become the opening line or a second finding.
</order_options>

<contrasting_examples>
Three mails for three invented prospects, to show how far apart two good mails can be. The facts in them are made up for the example. In real mails every fact comes from the research.

**Way in: the person. Order: person, story, gift, call. Dutch.**

```
Onderwerp: voor de werkplaats

Hoi Ruben,

Je hebt het vak geleerd bij je vader en staat nu zelf in dezelfde werkplaats aan de Havenstraat. Op je site staat daar één regel over, onderaan.

Ik heb er een nieuwe site omheen gebouwd, met jullie eigen foto's van de werkbank: [url]

Kijk er rustig naar, het kost je niets. Donderdag bel ik even om te horen wat je ervan vindt.
```

**Way in: the thing made for them. Order: gift, story, gap, call. English.**

```
Subject: a site for the bakery

Hi Marta,

There's a site waiting for you at [url]. It opens on the oven, because that is what your regulars talk about in every review I read.

Your current homepage starts with the opening hours and never gets to the bread.

It's free to look at and yours to keep thinking about. I'll ring Tuesday morning, after the first bake.
```

**Way in: their own words. Order: story, gift with the gap inside, call. English.**

```
Subject: "nothing frozen, ever"

Hi Daan,

"Nothing frozen, ever" is on your chalkboard and on the back of your menu. It deserves to be the first thing anyone sees.

I made a site that starts there, with your menu as text instead of a photo of it: [url]

No cost, no catch. I'll call this week to hear whether it feels like yours.
```

What differs between them: the first sentence, the position of the link, where the gap sits, how the call is announced, the subject line. That is the amount of difference the variation rule asks for.
</contrasting_examples>

<phrases_not_to_recycle>
These came from the accepted draft and the examples. They are used up.

- "Over the past two weeks I studied"
- "stuck with me"
- "is a story your current site barely tells"
- "So I built you a new one"
- "It's yours to look at and costs you nothing"
- "to hear what you think"
- "No cost, no catch"
- "There's a site waiting for you"

Say the same things in words that fit this prospect. "Free" can be said a dozen ways; so can "I will call".
</phrases_not_to_recycle>

<truth_checks>
Before saving the draft, check each sentence that is about Jaymar:

- Did he do this? If the user did not say so and the research does not show it, cut it.
- Is a time frame named ("two weeks", "last month")? Only if the user gave it.
- Is a day promised for the call? Only if the user gave it. Otherwise "this week", and tell him it is his promise.
- Does it claim a visit, a meal or a conversation? Only if the user said it happened.

Sentences about the prospect need a source too: their site, their menu, a named article. No source, no sentence.
</truth_checks>

<follow_up_call>
The mail announces the call, so the user needs what he will dial. After saving the draft, give him in chat:

- The phone number, and the hours they pick up, if the research has them.
- The one detail from the mail he can open the call with.
- Which older drafts to delete, when the draft tool cannot delete.
</follow_up_call>
